## Blur a Passport or ID Before You Send It — Here's the Safe Way

Landlords, banks, employers and apps all ask for a copy of your ID. Before that passport, driver's licence or national ID leaves your device, the responsible move is to **redact it** — blur or cover the parts the recipient doesn't need. You don't need a paid app: you can blur an ID online in your browser in a minute, and the original never has to leave your machine.

## Why You Should Redact an ID Copy

A full, unedited ID scan carries your document number, address, date of birth and sometimes a QR code or machine-readable zone (MRZ) that encodes all of it. Sent to the wrong inbox or leaked from a data breach, that's enough for identity theft. Most requests only need to *confirm* who you are, not see every field — so share the minimum.

## What to Mask on an ID (Quick Checklist)

Work from a copy, never the original scan, and hide anything the recipient didn't ask for:

- **Full document number** — keep only the last 4 digits if a reference is needed
- **QR code / barcode** — often encodes the same personal data as the printed fields
- **Home address** — share only if the process explicitly requires it
- **Signature** — avoid sending unless mandatory
- **MRZ (passport)** — the two machine-readable lines at the bottom
- **Any national ID / social security number** visible on the page

Rule of thumb: if a field isn't required for verification, mask it.

## How to Blur an ID Online (Safe Workflow)

1. **Make a copy first.** Duplicate the scan and work only on the copy; keep the original untouched.
2. **Open it in a browser editor** such as [Blur Image Online](/blur-image) — no install, runs on phone and desktop.
3. **Paint over the sensitive fields** with the brush or rectangle tool. For short strings like a document number, [Blur Text in Image](/blur-text) targets the characters directly.
4. **Use a solid cover for the strongest hiding**, or a strong blur/pixelation for fields you want obscured but not fully blacked out.
5. **Download only the redacted copy.** The original stays on your device.

For a one-pass pass, [Automatic Privacy Blur](/privacy-blur) can flag candidate faces, plates and text areas so you can confirm what to hide.

## A Watermark Is Not Enough on Its Own

A watermark ("Copy for X Bank only — 26 Sept 2026") ties the document to one recipient and one use, which is good practice. But it **hides nothing** — the number underneath is still readable. Always cover what the recipient doesn't need *first*, then add the watermark on top of what's left.

## After You Send It

- **Rename the file** before sending if its name leaks something private (e.g. `passport_jane_doe.jpg`).
- **Delete extra copies** from your phone, downloads folder and email sent items once the task is closed.
- If you sent an unmasked copy by mistake, ask the recipient to delete it and confirm in writing, and watch your accounts for anything unusual.

## FAQ

### Can I blur an ID on my phone?
Yes. Browser-based tools run on Android and iOS with no app install, so you can redact the ID on the same device you received the request on.

### Does blurring change the original file?
No. Redaction is done locally in your browser; you download a processed copy and the original never leaves your device as part of the normal workflow.

### Is a blur or a solid box better for ID numbers?
For maximum safety on highly sensitive numbers, a solid cover block is strongest. A strong blur or pixelation is acceptable for less critical fields, but always zoom in to 100% and confirm the text can't be read.

### Can I refuse to send a full ID copy?
Often, yes. Many organisations can confirm identity another way (a customer number, a login, or a partial redacted copy). If a request feels excessive, ask why the copy is needed and what they'll do with it.
