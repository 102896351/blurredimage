## Blur Image for GDPR: A Practical Way to Hide Personal Data

If a photo you are about to post contains someone's face, a number plate, a name or a screen with account details, that picture may count as **personal data** under the GDPR. The safe habit is simple: **blur image for GDPR** purposes before it leaves your device. You do not need legal training to do the right thing — you need a quick editing pass that hides the identifiers.

## Why Images Fall Under the GDPR

The GDPR protects any information that relates to an identifiable person. A raw photo often carries more than the subject:

- Faces and bystanders in the background
- Number plates on cars and delivery vehicles
- Names, emails and order numbers in screenshots
- Addresses on package labels
- Account details in app captures

Once such an image is published, removing it is hard. Blurring or redacting the sensitive parts first is the low-cost step that prevents the problem.

## What to Redact Before Sharing

A good **blur image for GDPR** workflow covers these areas:

1. **Faces** — use [Blur Face Online](/blur-face) for group photos, events and street shots.
2. **Text and fields** — use [Blur Text in Image](/blur-text) for screenshots, order numbers and forms.
3. **Licence plates** — use [Blur Licence Plate Online](/blur-license-plate) for vehicles and used-car photos.
4. **Whole documents** — [Automatic Privacy Blur](/privacy-blur) can detect candidate faces, plates and text areas so you can confirm what to hide.

For anything that must disappear completely, a solid cover block works better than a soft blur. Pixelation is a good middle ground for short strings such as names or plates.

## A Quick GDPR Image Checklist

Before you hit publish, ask:

- Is there a face that is not the intended subject? → blur it
- Does a plate or vehicle appear? → redact it
- Are names, emails or numbers visible? → cover them
- Is a location or address readable? → hide it
- Could any detail identify a person indirectly? → blur to be safe

You can run this check with [Blur Image Online](/blur-image), which keeps the original on your device and exports only a processed copy.

## Does Blurring Alone Make an Image Compliant?

Blurring helps, but it is not a magic switch. The GDPR is about necessity and context: share the minimum that serves your purpose, keep identifiers out, and avoid publishing more than needed. A strong blur or pixelation is usually enough to hide faces, emails and casual screenshot details, but for highly sensitive records consider a more conservative approach and store the clean original only where it belongs.

## FAQ

### Can I blur image for GDPR on mobile?
Yes. The tools run in the browser, so they work on Android and iOS without any app install.

### Is blurring enough to anonymise a photo?
For everyday sharing, a strong blur or pixelation is enough to hide faces, names and plates. For sensitive records, also remove the context that could identify someone.

### Do my images leave the device?
No. Blurring happens locally in your browser, so images are not uploaded as part of the normal workflow.
