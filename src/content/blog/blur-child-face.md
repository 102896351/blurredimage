## Blur Your Child's Face Before You Post — A Sharenting Safety Guide

Posting photos of your kids is natural, but once a picture is public it can be copied, saved and rebuilt by people you'll never meet. A simple habit — **blurring your child's face** before it goes online — removes the single most identifying detail. You don't need a fancy app: you can blur a face in your browser in seconds, and the photo never has to leave your device.

## Why It Matters (the "Sharenting" Risk)

Privacy regulators and paediatric bodies are clear about the downsides of over-sharing:

- A child's **image rights** are increasingly protected by law — in France, a 2024 law reinforced that publishing a child's photo is not routine and needs the consent of *both* parents.
- Everyday photos can be collected and misused by strangers, including to build profiles or generate fake explicit material.
- Once posted, a photo is hard to fully delete — even if you remove it later, copies may remain.

You don't have to stop sharing. You just share more carefully.

## What to Hide Besides the Face

Blurring the face is the start. Before posting, also check for:

- **The face** — blur it, shoot from behind, or crop to a hand or back.
- **School name, logo or uniform** — these pinpoint a location.
- **Geotags and check-ins** — turn off location sharing; they reveal where you are.
- **Full name and age** — avoid pairing all three with a photo.
- **Private moments** — skip swimsuit or bath photos entirely.

A useful rule: never put **full name + specific location + identifiable photo** in the same post. Drop any one of the three and the risk drops sharply.

## How to Blur a Child's Face Online

1. **Open the photo** in a browser editor such as [Blur Face Online](/blur-face) — no install, works on phone and desktop.
2. **Auto-detect or brush** over the face. [Blur Face Online](/blur-face) can find faces in one pass; for a single shot, [Blur Image Online](/blur-image) lets you paint a rectangle or freehand over the area.
3. **Pick the effect** — a solid cover or strong blur for the face, lighter blur for the background if you want it softened.
4. **Download the redacted copy** — the original stays on your device.

For group photos and events, [Automatic Privacy Blur](/privacy-blur) can flag every face, plate and text area so you can confirm what to hide.

## Account and Sharing Settings

Blurring helps, but pair it with platform settings:

- **Keep the account private** and share only with people you know.
- Use **Close Friends / selected groups** so kids' photos don't go to all followers.
- **Sort your followers** and remove people you don't recognise.
- Ask family and friends **not to repost** your child's photo without checking first.
- Periodically **delete old posts** that are no longer relevant.

## FAQ

### Can I blur a child's face on my phone?
Yes. Browser-based tools run on Android and iOS with no app install, so you can redact the photo on the device you took it with.

### Does blurring change the original file?
No. Redaction is done locally in your browser; you download a processed copy and the original never leaves your device as part of the normal workflow.

### Is a blur or a solid block better for a face?
For everyday sharing, a strong blur or pixelation hides a face well. If the photo is very sensitive, a solid cover block is the safest choice.

### Do I need the other parent's permission?
In many places, yes — publishing a child's image is treated as a decision needing both parents' consent. When in doubt, blur the face and keep the post to a private, trusted audience.
